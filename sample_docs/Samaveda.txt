DOCUMENT_ID: VEDA-02-SAMAVEDA
TITLE: Samaveda Samhita - Chants, Melody, and Liturgical Role
CATEGORY: Sacred Hindu Texts / Vedic Literature
CHRONOLOGY: Vedic Period (circa 1200-900 BCE)
LANGUAGE: Vedic Sanskrit

[SECTION 1: PURPOSE AND MUSICAL CHARACTER]
The Samaveda is the Veda of melodies and chants, derived from the Sanskrit root "saman" meaning song or melody. Rather than serving primarily as an independent collection of new verse, the Samaveda adapts verses—overwhelmingly drawn from the Rigveda—and sets them to specific melodic patterns (ganas) to be sung by the Udgatr priests during Soma sacrifices.

[SECTION 2: COLLECTION STRUCTURE]
The Samaveda is organized into two main divisions: the Purvarchika (containing verses arranged by deity or ritual context) and the Uttararchika (containing longer song sequences structured for continuous liturgical performance). Musical notation and chant instructions—such as the Kauthuma, Ranayaniya, and Jaiminiya recensions—preserve ancient vocal inflection and pitch markers used to guide ritual singing.

[SECTION 3: SOMA RITUAL AND LITURGICAL PERFORMANCE]
Chanting in the Samaveda is closely tied to the preparation and offering of Soma, a central ritual drink in Vedic liturgy. Priests perform chants in groups, using specific tonal registers, syllables, and rhythmic cadences that transform spoken mantras into structured sacred music.

[SECTION 4: INFLUENCE ON INDIAN CLASSICAL MUSIC]
Because the Samaveda systematically organizes pitch, scale degrees (svaras), and melodic motifs within a sacred framework, historians of music regard it as an early root of Indian classical musical traditions. Over time, its chant conventions also influenced philosophical inquiry, with the Chandogya Upanishad attached to the Samaveda tradition.
