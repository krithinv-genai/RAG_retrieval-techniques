DOCUMENT_ID: VEDA-03-YAJURVEDA
TITLE: Yajurveda Samhita - Ritual Formulas, Sacrifice, and Prose
CATEGORY: Sacred Hindu Texts / Vedic Literature
CHRONOLOGY: Middle to Late Vedic Period (circa 1200-800 BCE)
LANGUAGE: Vedic Sanskrit

[SECTION 1: LITURGICAL FORMULAS AND PRIESTLY ROLE]
The Yajurveda is the Veda of sacrificial formulas ("yajus"), providing prose mantras and instructions used primarily by the Adhvaryu priest, who performs the physical actions and measurements of a ritual. While the Rigveda focuses on praise and invocation and the Samaveda on melodic chanting, the Yajurveda focuses on procedural execution—preparing the fire altar, handling utensils, and consecrating offerings.

[SECTION 2: SHUKLA (WHITE) AND KRISHNA (BLACK) YAJURVEDA]
The Yajurveda divides into two major branches:
- Shukla Yajurveda (White Yajurveda), such as the Vajasaneyi Samhita, keeps the mantra verses cleanly separated from explanatory prose commentary (the Shatapatha Brahmana).
- Krishna Yajurveda (Black Yajurveda), such as the Taittiriya Samhita, blends sacrificial formulas directly with prose explanations and ritual commentary within the same text body.

[SECTION 3: SACRIFICIAL RITES AND GEOMETRY]
The text details domestic and public rituals, including new and full moon sacrifices, fire-laying ceremonies (Agnyadhana), and elaborate altar constructions (Shulba traditions in later sutras). Precise spatial arrangement and exact recitation are treated as essential to maintaining ritual order and efficacy.

[SECTION 4: TRANSITION TO PHILOSOPHICAL INQUIRY]
The concluding portion of the Shukla Yajurveda is the Isha Upanishad, which shifts attention from external ritual mechanics toward innerrenunciation, knowledge, and action in the world. This placement illustrates how ritual manuals gradually gave rise to metaphysical reflection.
